**How to read an entry.** Each entry gives the question, the decision, the alternatives rejected and why, the recorded decision it revises (D- or E- numbers in `docs/audit/DECISIONS.md`), the findings it gates (severity in brackets: C Critical, H High, M Medium, L Low) and its sources, each a URL or repository path with the passage relied on. "Plan" names the batch plan section where the full text and tests live; where this summary and a plan differ, the plan is the authority, except for the two conflicts this file resolved (section 3), where both plans were edited to agree.

**Principles that recur.** Several decisions apply the same few rules. Naming them once makes the entries shorter:
- **P1. A figure carries only what its source establishes.** Money is money only in a currency the run holds evidence for (D-28); a period is established only by what the record itself carries (XBRL 2.1 §4.6.1 "All items MUST have a context"; §4.7.2 the period is an instant or "a period beginning and ending as specified", re-checked 2026-10-28, https://www.xbrl.org/Specification/XBRL-2.1/REC-2003-12-31/XBRL-2.1-REC-2003-12-31+corrected-errata-2013-02-20.html). What cannot be established is withheld or left unchecked, with the reason shown, never guessed.
- **P2. A stricter check that marks more claims unchecked is intended.** A claim is attributable only if "all parts can be directly inferred from the source" (Rashkin et al., AIS, https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12870). Recall losses are measured and listed; they are never fixed by loosening.
- **P3. Count every claim once, on one basis.** Income and the capital that earns it sit on the same side of the valuation (Damodaran, "Holdings in Other Firms": "If you do not do so, you will double count the value of the subsidiary", https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/valcrosshold.htm).
- **P4. Spend errs high, never low.** An over-counted presumption is visible and reconciled; an under-count breaks the caps (D-07: "over-counts until reconciled, which is the safe direction").
- **P5. Stored reports keep their bytes (D-67).** A fix that changes what an old report means gets a display-time rule keyed on a frozen spec-version cutoff, and says once that a correction was applied.
- **P6. Say a disclosure once, where the reader needs it.** GOV.UK Design System, Notification banner: "Avoid showing more than one notification banner on the same page. Instead, combine the messages in a single notification banner." (re-checked 2026-10-28, https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/notification-banner/); 17 CFR 230.421(b) Note: avoid "Disclosure repeated in different sections of the document that increases the size of the document but does not enhance the quality of the information" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/230.421).

## 2. Decisions by topic

### 2.1 Claim verification: periods and windows (B1)

**B1-OD-1. A period-less record establishes only what it carries.** Plan: B1 OD-1. Gates: AI-106 (H), AI-303 (H), AI-303-V1 (H).
- *Question.* When a claim names a fiscal year, quarter, date or window and cites a record whose `period` is null (181 of 940 rows in NVDA #50), or a dated record without fiscal metadata, does the claim stay unchecked, or do those records gain a fiscal label?
- *Decision.* Verifier-only, no payload change. A period-less record establishes its own `asOf`, a date its `periodContext` declares (`at`, or a duration's `end`), a fiscal label it declares (`fiscalPeriod` or `periodContext.fiscalLabel`, matched only when the claim's period reads as an FY label), and windows its own label names (D-66). Anything else named against it leaves the claim unverified (`period-mismatch`), never validated or contradicted (F-22). A dated record without fiscal metadata establishes its date only. Measured cost at planning: NVDA #48's "ROIC for the latest fiscal year" and "accrual ratio for the same year" move from validated to unverified. With B3 Task 8E merged first, both stay validated and only "ROIC for FY2025 was 149.8%." moves (section 3, C-11).
- *Rejected.* (a) Give `(latest)` computed rows a fiscal label now: right in principle, but a payload change that belongs with payload work; it can follow without reopening this rule. It now does: B3 Task 8E gives those rows a label from the date Stage B used (4.3 residual (a); section 2.3). (b) Infer "annual" when a row's as-of equals a fiscal year end: an inference, wrong for a TTM row that happens to end at a year end.
- *Revises.* No D-entry; restores the F-22 owner decision (`DECISIONS.md:2250`) that commit `2b77104` broke (AI-106 is a regression).
- *Sources.* XBRL 2.1 §4.6.1 and §4.7.2 (P1). Rashkin et al. (P2). 17 CFR 210.10-01(c)(1): a 10-Q carries "An interim balance sheet as of the end of the most recent fiscal quarter" and "a balance sheet as of the end of the preceding fiscal year", so one filing registers several periods for the same line (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/210.10-01).

**B1-OD-2. A trailing window's start date counts only inside the complete interval.** Plan: B1 OD-2. Gates: AI-302 (H).
- *Decision.* A lone date names a window's end. A TTM or YTD start date agrees only when the claim also states the end date, per D-82's interval-proof principle.
- *Rejected.* Admitting the start after "from/since/beginning": "since 2025-07-27" states no end, and the window start is often also the prior-year quarter end that other rows carry, which is the slip the check must catch.
- *Revises.* D-59's start-date allowance (commit `149e18d`); extends D-82.
- *Sources.* XBRL 2.1 §4.7.2: a duration is "A period beginning and ending as specified" (re-checked 2026-10-28). 17 CFR 210.10-01(c)(2): interim statements cover "the period between the end of the preceding fiscal year and the end of the most recent fiscal quarter".

**B1-OD-3. A forward-looking clause is never read as a reported result.** Plan: B1 OD-3. Gates: AI-302-V1 (H). Decided together with B2-OD-1 (default 3).
- *Decision.* On the excerpt path a forward-looking source clause ("is/are expected to", "expects", "anticipates", "projected/forecast/estimated/guided to", "outlook", and management guidance: "guidance for/of/is/was/issued/range", "our/its/management's … guidance") inherits no period or window from the excerpt title, and the claim's modality must match. Accounting-standard guidance is not forward-looking.
- *Rejected.* (a) The inheritance rule alone: a guided figure stated as a past result would still validate. (b) Carrying an outlook lead's period across its bullets: a recall gain only, left as a follow-up. (c) Leaving out the noun "guidance" (first draft): "Our guidance for third-quarter revenue is $108.0 billion." in a Q2 release still validated "Revenue for Q2 FY2027 was USD 108.0 billion." at HEAD.
- *Revises.* D-63 ("a clause naming no period takes the excerpt's own").
- *Sources.* 15 U.S.C. §78u-5(i)(1)(A): a forward-looking statement is "a statement containing a projection of revenues, income … or other financial items" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78u-5). Saurí and Pustejovsky, FactBank, LREC 2008: systems must "recognize whether events are presented as corresponding to real situations in the world, situations that have not happened, or situations of uncertain status" (https://www.cs.brandeis.edu/~roser/pubs/sauriPustejovsky_lrec08_2.pdf). The committed NVIDIA Q2 FY2027 release fixture labels its outlook as forward-looking statements under Section 27A / 21E.

**B1 calls without a recorded owner decision** (recorded in B1's D-next entry). Gates: AI-402 (H), AI-402-V1 (M), AI-402-V2 (L), AI-404 (M), AI-303-V3 (H), AI-303-V4 (H), AI-405 (M).
- *C-1 Comparison-base cues.* "compared with/to", "versus", "vs.", "relative to", "against", "than", "above", "below"; "from/over" after a change word; a bare "from" only across an amount (D-98 shape). A period directly after a bare "from/over" is never a base; an amount range is not a base. `src/edgar/currentReports.ts` is not changed (B9 owns known issue B1-3); reviewers must not "align" the two grammars in either batch. Source: D-98 (`DECISIONS.md:2081`); Amazon Q2 2026 EX-99.1 sentence "AWS segment operating income was $16.6 billion, compared with $10.2 billion in second quarter 2025."
- *C-2 A base is checked as a base* on both paths; an FY-labelled base is compared only with the shifted fiscal label (review found the calendar-year comparison gave NVDA's correct "compared with Q2 FY2026" a false finding).
- *C-3 A one-figure claim is read whole* (extends D-82).
- *C-4 Fiscal year-end and "latest".* The fiscal year end is established only by an FY/Q4 label or fiscal-year basis; "latest" requires no later registered period of the series. Source: 17 CFR 210.10-01(c)(1) distinguishes "the end of the most recent fiscal quarter" from "the end of the preceding fiscal year".
- *C-5 The period check is never stricter than the citation check.* Spelled fiscal periods are compared through `periodsAgree`. Source: 26 CFR 1.441-2(a)(1) and (c)(1), 52-53-week years deemed to end on the last day of the nearest calendar month (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.441-2).
- *C-6 Excerpt windows compared as periods* (extends D-63's inheritance to windows).

### 2.2 Claim verification: numbers, binding and sources (B2)

**B2-OD-1. An excerpt figure binds only to its own description.** Plan: B2 OD-1. Gates: AI-305 (H), AI-305-V1 (M). Default 3.
- *Decision.* Keep D-63's clause subset test and also require that every content word of the source figure's own description is named by the claim. The description runs from the clause start, the previous figure or the last unclosed bracket, colon or semicolon up to the figure, plus the words after it up to the first comma, semicolon, colon, parenthesis or dash; a table cell's is its row lead. Content words exclude grammar, period descriptors, ordinals, comparison-basis phrases, the issuer name and a closed context-word list; nouns, adjectives, modifiers, comparators and direction words are never excluded. Being a conjunction with HEAD's rule, it never validates anything HEAD did not.
- *Rejected.* (a) Keep D-63: its clause-wide subset test is the "lexical overlap" heuristic, and it validated four wrong metrics on real excerpts of #43, #46 and #50, two of them visible as green key-number checks. (b) A syntactic parser: a new dependency with probabilistic output. (c) A symmetric whole-clause test: trailing explanations would fail almost every correct figure.
- *Revises.* D-63 (recall over precision, `claimEvidence.ts:456-464`) and D-45's accepted same-sentence residual; D-66's required GAAP and net modifiers are kept.
- *Sources.* McCoy, Pavlick and Linzen, "Right for the Wrong Reasons", ACL 2019: the lexical overlap heuristic, "Assume that a premise entails all hypotheses constructed from words in the premise." (https://aclanthology.org/P19-1334/). Harper et al., SemEval-2021 Task 8 MeasEval: a quantity is read with its own measured entity, property and qualifier (https://aclanthology.org/2021.semeval-1.38/). Rashkin et al. 2023, Computational Linguistics 49(4): attributable when a generic hearer affirms "According to P, s" (https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/).

**B2-OD-2. A registry-cited figure printed under a description naming another metric.** Plan: B2 OD-2. Gates: AI-407 (M).
- *Decision.* Fail closed on a detected conflict (unverified, never contradicted): a differing level or share basis, a one-sided non-GAAP/adjusted/core basis, a segment record whose member is not named, or disjoint head words under a closed alias table; a printed source id different from the matched record is also unverified. No label substitution.
- *Rejected.* Substituting the record's label (shows text the judge never wrote); full symmetric agreement (true paraphrases lose check marks); "shares no token" (the first draft: unverifies every macro row such as "Ten-year Treasury yield" against "DGS10 (core)").
- *Revises.* Extends D-44 to every printed description; amends the `provenance.ts:278-283` design note.
- *Sources.* Rashkin et al. 2023 (above); internal precedent D-44 and `namedUnitMatchesRecord` (`passes.ts:3022`).

**B2-OD-3. A MetricRow cell's column period, and supplied periods on period-less records.** Plan: B2 OD-3 (edited by the integration, conflict C-2). Gates: AI-408 (M).
- *Decision.* A bare ISO-date or bare fiscal-label column must be established by the cited record, else `period-mismatch` (unverified). A date must equal the record's period or, when the period is null, a date the record carries (as-of, or its `periodContext` instant or duration end), the same set as B1-OD-1. A fiscal label must agree through `periodsAgree` with `fiscalPeriod`, else `periodContext.fiscalLabel`; a record with no fiscal label cannot establish one. Every other column is "not period-checked", and the log says so. A supplied period on a period-less record is erased only when it does not decidably conflict.
- *Rejected.* Failing every unreadable column (stored reports use "current" and "TTM to 2026-07-26" on true cells); copying the record's period into every number (clutter against R1–R4).
- *Revises.* The `10aa8ab` rule that erases every supplied period (`passes.ts:3027-3043`); corrects METHODOLOGY.md about lines 2575-2576.
- *Sources.* XBRL 2.1 glossary, "Context": contexts "document the entity, the period and the scenario that collectively give the appropriate context for understanding the values of items" (URL in P1). Rashkin et al. 2023.

**B2-OD-4. Which sources may support naming an issuer's own drugs, trials and deals.** Plan: B2 OD-4 (redesigned in review). Gates: AI-103 (M).
- *Decision.* On the judge path a source is primary when it is a registered URL or an issuer-originated disclosure the payload carries: `edgar:` excerpts and citations, the earnings-call transcript, `fmp:press-release`, payload and computed registry records (an issuer-prefix allowlist). *Confirmed (4.3, 2026-10-28).* OD-2 rule 5, OD-4's issuer-prefix allowlist and OD-5's tighter mask, each stricter than the first draft and each checked by the reviewer, are confirmed. Each gains a "Confirmed" paragraph in the plan.

**B2-OD-6. Which percentage-point records are signed changes (round 4).** Plan: B2 OD-6, Task 19. Gates: AI-502 (M).
- *Question.* `isDeltaRecord` treats every percentage-point record as a signed change, so a movement verb is checked against the sign of a level difference. Should signed differences keep a comparative sign check while true levels get none, and should the kind come from the payload or from the verifier?
- *Decision.* A percentage-point record is a signed change only when it measures a change over time. That means its unit is `percentage-points-per-year` (a slope), or its unit is `percentage-points`, its final id segment names a change (growth, cagr, change, delta, chg, yoy, qoq, expansion, contraction, revision) and its label names no two operands joined by a difference operator (" − ", " - ", "minus", "vs", "versus", "over"). Every other percentage-point record is a same-date difference or a magnitude. Examples are the ROIC − WACC spread and its score drivers, T10Y2Y and T10Y3M, the terminal excess return, relative strength vs SPY, actual vs prior guidance midpoint, the absolute accrual divergence and revenue acceleration (YoY − 3y CAGR). Against such a record no direction word is read. A claim with a movement verb or a comparative beside the figure stays unchecked (`text-context-mismatch`), never validated or contradicted. A signed difference's sign is still checked through its value. The classification lives in the verifier. A pin test over every percentage-point record in the test payload fails CI until a new row is classified, and an unlisted row defaults to "not a change", the fail-closed side.
- *Rejected.* (a) Keep a postposed comparative ("1.03 pp lower") as a sign check for "vs" differences: "lower" can mean lower than the second operand or lower than before, and the movement reading is the defect. No stored claim has the shape (scan of the 52 stored verification logs), so the loss is recorded as a recall residual. (b) A producer-set kind field on every registry record, the finding's sketch and the 4.1 lean: it would move PAYLOAD_VERSION and the golden's `provenanceHash` in a batch that changes no payload data, and would add `payload.ts` to B2's files. The unit, slug and label already say what each row measures, and the pin test gives the same robustness. (c) Keep HEAD: it validates movements no record measured and contradicts true level sentences, against F-22.
- *Revises.* The percentage-point shortcut in `isDeltaRecord` (D-20 WS7; F186 docstring, `consistency.ts:646-663`). It narrows what F-22 (`DECISIONS.md:2250`) counts as "its cited change". The 4.1 lean is followed in outcome and replaced in mechanism, so PAYLOAD_VERSION does not move in B2.
- *Sources.* Eurostat, Statistics Explained, "Glossary: Percentage point": "The term percentage point is used when comparing two different percentages", so the unit alone does not say whether a record is a movement or a spread (checked 2026-10-28, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Percentage_point). FRED T10Y2Y: "10-Year Treasury Constant Maturity Minus 2-Year Treasury Constant Maturity … calculated as the spread", a daily level series (checked 2026-10-28, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y2Y). Rashkin et al. 2023 (P2). Internal: F-22, and `payload.ts:945` registers the divergence as an absolute value.

### 2.3 Payload records and peers (B3; peer items in B6 and B9)

**B3-OD-1. Peer market-cap currency.** Plan: B3 OD-1. Gates: **AI-301 (C)**, AI-301-V1 (L). Default 1.
- *Question.* Should peer caps inherit the subject's currency at all, only for same-exchange listings, and should each peer's profile currency be fetched?
