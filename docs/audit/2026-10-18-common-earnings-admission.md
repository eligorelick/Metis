# Common-earnings admission — 2026-10-18

This separate stage addresses earnings ownership and flow scope. D-84 cash-dividend admission is retained unchanged. No live issuer/model requests, production database changes, taxonomy bridge ingestion, or historical report rewrites are involved.

## Accounting basis

[SEC SAB Topic 6.B](https://www.sec.gov/interps/account/sabcodet6.htm) includes unpaid cumulative dividends and preferred carrying-value accretions when determining common income. Preferred cash paid cannot establish that earnings allocation.

[FASB ASU 2018-09](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU_2018-09.pdf), printed pp.13–14 (PDF pp.18–19), ASC 260-10-45-60B/55-62, requires allocation to participating securities. Example 6 supplies NI 65,000, preferred cash 27,000, and common cash 26,000. Applying its participation terms to undistributed income 12,000 allocates 3,000 to preferred and 9,000 to common, yielding common income 35,000. That arithmetic is an inference from the example, not a new retained issuer fact.

The synthetic unsupported cases therefore refuse to compute common earnings: NI 100/cash 4 (a complete entitlement bridge could support 92), cash 12 including arrears (could support 92), cash 8 beside accretion (could support 90), and the two-class example (could support 35,000). The adapter has no complete bridge contract or source ingestion for those cases; it does not guess the illustrative supported values.

## Implementation boundary

`securityBasis.selectCommonIncome` owns the shared earnings decision. `returns.incomeAvailableToCommon`, ROTE, payout denominators, and `securityBasis.commonHolderCapital` use the same guard. Finite direct common income retains zero and negative values without another preferred/NCI deduction. A preferred claim or nonzero preferred cash prevents using total income as common income. Signed NCI allocations retain their existing ownership guards.

`compute.ts` carries per-field issuer/currency/start/end/basis into annual returns, payout joins, current multiples, TTM common income, and historical quarter inputs. An explicit incompatible flow interval is refused. Nearby display dates are candidates only when retained field evidence establishes the same actual interval. Unknown vendor starts retain their sourced annual contract; they are not invented dates or automatic conflicts. Balance-instant tolerance and averaging calculations are unchanged. Preferred exposure is retained separately from currency arithmetic so a partial or incompatible-currency cash observation cannot disappear into an assumed absence of preferred claims. Actual cash rows remain intact.

Known annual/TTM intervals must span 350-380 days (including 52/53-week years); known quarters must span 60-120 days. These are bounded admission conventions for this flow selector, not an accounting standard or a change to universal statement normalization. Matching six-month stubs therefore cannot establish an annual numerator. The bundle remains issuer-local upstream; optional known issuer IDs are compared without requiring new metadata absent from the vendor contract.

TTM construction retains coherent ownership and signed NCI allocations. Explicit consolidated income requires all four quarterly allocations, even with zero closing NCI equity. Mixed or partly missing ownership refuses the total-income fallback. Nonfinite allocations are refused before arithmetic. Parent income is not adjusted again; independently admitted filed common income remains authoritative even when the fallback is refused.

Outside that tested TTM construction, the existing annual selector can still infer zero NCI allocation when closing NCI equity is zero and no allocation is supplied. Closing equity alone cannot prove in-period NCI absence; expanding this inference guard requires separate in-period exposure evidence and regressions. This stage does not claim to resolve every ownership-data limitation.

The follow-up review exposed aggregation gaps hidden by plausible row dates and outer TTM bounds. TTM now checks each retained earnings field's actual quarter end against its candidate row date (the existing five-day candidate tolerance), strictly ordered actual ends, and exact adjacent-day coverage where starts are known. Duplicate quarters, overlaps, gaps, and conflicting known field currencies/issuer IDs refuse that field's earnings fallback before aggregation. Contiguous intervals, genuinely nearby display dates with compatible actual intervals, and unknown vendor starts retain their prior admission. Missing starts do not establish independently verified coverage. Known nonzero signed NCI income with unknown parent/consolidated ownership also refuses the total-income fallback, even with zero closing NCI equity; this does not guess a deduction or affect independently admitted common earnings.

Known `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic` provenance is labeled as the filed Basic EPS common-stockholder numerator used by the current house return convention. ROTE notes disclose that participating-security allocations can differ from issuer common-equity earnings. Those admitted numeric values, including zero and negative values, are preserved. Unknown vendor allocation conventions remain labeled unknown. This stage does not reconstruct an EPS-to-common-equity bridge or redesign ROTE/TCE. A broader convention change requires separate evidence, scope, and positive controls.

## Deterministic source timing and historical allocation completeness

A subsequent synthetic review reproduced a host-time-zone dependency: direct common income 94 with unzoned filing `2026-07-06 16:00:00` and cutoff `2026-07-06T20:00:00Z` was admitted in UTC and withheld in Pacific time. [ECMA-262 Date.parse](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/numbers-and-dates.html#sec-date.parse) specifies host-local interpretation for offset-free date-time forms; nonconforming formats can invoke implementation-specific parsing. The checked [FMP income-statement documentation](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/stable/income-statement) did not provide an `acceptedDate` time-zone contract. This work does not infer a provider time zone from that absence.

The selector now parses source clock components and explicit offsets deterministically after validating the calendar day and clock. Explicit-offset/Z source timestamps remain instants. Source date-only values and valid unzoned date-times retain calendar-day precision under the bounded house day-evidence convention already used in `pipeline/peers.ts`; their clock text does not establish intraday availability. An older completed evidence day remains admissible. Same-day imprecise filing availability requires precise observation of that same field by the cutoff. A precise filing for one component cannot prove another component's day-only availability. Raw vendor acceptance is retained separately and can prove its own row's filing availability when it supplies an explicit instant; it is never relabeled as observation.

Independent per-field SEC provenance takes precedence over unused row filing/acceptance metadata. An ambiguous, future, or malformed unused vendor row timestamp cannot veto valid primary field evidence. Malformed primary field timestamps still refuse. An imprecise same-day observation cannot be rescued by a prior filing. Explicit future observations, filings, or acceptances refuse. Empty legacy `observedAt` placeholders remain missing evidence rather than invented instants. Calendar granularity cannot establish full intraday point-in-time availability, and this admission convention does not claim a provider time zone or change source-recency logic elsewhere.

Cutoff semantics are preserved: a valid date-only cutoff remains **UTC start-of-day**, an explicit-offset/Z cutoff denotes its exact instant, and an unzoned date-time cutoff is unsupported. Calendar rollovers such as February 30, invalid clocks/offsets, and malformed cutoffs refuse. No global date framework or default cutoff changes are introduced.

The historical P/E path also now uses the current TTM ownership/allocation completeness guard. Explicit consolidated fallback income requires all four signed NCI allocations even when closing NCI equity is zero. Missing current or older allocations drop unsupported historical observations; complete allocations and independently admitted direct common earnings remain positive controls. This fixes the separately reproduced historical values 58.4795 and 77.5194 without changing cash data, clean TCE, or the admitted Basic numerator convention. The annual closing-zero legacy inference limitation described above remains.

[17 CFR 210.5-03, captions 18-20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-210/section-210.5-03) separately identifies net income or loss, income attributable to NCI, and income attributable to the controlling interest. This supports retaining ownership attribution rather than silently treating consolidated earnings as parent earnings. Requiring complete signed allocations across a fallback TTM window is this application's engineering admission inference; the regulation does not prescribe that aggregation algorithm.
